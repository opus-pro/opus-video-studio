# Template licensing and asset notices

The MIT grant in this directory covers Opus-authored animation source,
configuration, documentation, and template preparation tools. It does not
change the licensing of the plugin outside this directory or of third-party
content. Original release manifests and asset records are retained unchanged.

## Software

- Remotion and its packages retain their own license. Consult the license in
  the installed package and [Remotion licensing](https://www.remotion.dev/license).
- React, Three.js, React Three Fiber, TypeScript, and other dependencies retain
  their own licenses. Each project includes its original npm lockfile; installed
  packages carry their notices.

## Fonts

Geist, Instrument Serif, and Fraunces font files are restored from the original
release where used. Their SIL Open Font License notices are committed beside
the original asset paths or in the project's licenses directory. Preserve those
notices when redistributing fonts. System fonts are not bundled; their availability
can affect rendering on another operating system.

## Media and example brands

Images, music, narration, sound effects, reference previews, logos, and marks
are **not covered by the source-code MIT grant**. Their original provenance
is documented in each project's `ASSETS.md` or `ASSETS.json`, related source
records, and retained license files. This repository does not grant trademark
rights or additional rights to provider-generated outputs.

For example, Margin's ocean photograph is credited to Jeffrey Brandjes under
the Unsplash License in its asset record. Numo retains the GeneralUser GS
notice for the soundfont used in its audio production. Some supplied mixes
record incomplete provenance for individual sound effects; do not treat those
mixes as MIT-licensed or independently cleared assets.

Use the linked video demos as examples. When adapting a template for a new
product, supply branding, imagery, and audio that you have permission to use.
Several full templates include narration already mixed into their master
audio; changing on-screen copy alone does not replace that narration.
